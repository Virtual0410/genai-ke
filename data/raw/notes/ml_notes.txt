PAGE 1: INTRODUCTION TO ML IN PERSONNEL MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------
Machine Learning (ML) is increasingly used in personnel and human resource
management to support data-driven decision-making. Personnel ML focuses on
analyzing employee-related data to improve hiring, performance evaluation,
retention, and workforce planning.

Common data sources include resumes, application forms, performance reviews,
attendance logs, engagement surveys, payroll data, and training records.
These datasets may be structured (tables, scores) or unstructured (text,
emails, feedback).

Key objectives of personnel ML:
- Improve quality and speed of recruitment
- Reduce employee turnover
- Identify high-potential employees
- Detect bias and ensure fairness
- Optimize training and development programs

Challenges specific to personnel ML:
- Data privacy and legal compliance (GDPR, labor laws)
- Bias and discrimination risks
- Interpretability of models
- Ethical use of employee data

Typical ML task types:
- Classification (e.g., hire/no-hire, promotion readiness)
- Regression (e.g., performance score prediction)
- Clustering (e.g., employee segmentation)
- Natural Language Processing (resume screening, feedback analysis)

PAGE 2: RECRUITMENT AND TALENT ACQUISITION
-----------------------------------------
ML plays a major role in modern recruitment pipelines. Automated resume
screening systems use NLP techniques to extract skills, experience, and
education from resumes and compare them against job requirements.

Key techniques:
- Keyword extraction and embeddings
- Similarity scoring between resumes and job descriptions
- Ranking candidates based on predicted job fit

Common models:
- Logistic Regression
- Random Forests
- Gradient Boosting
- Transformer-based NLP models (for text-heavy tasks)

Benefits:
- Reduced time-to-hire
- Consistent candidate evaluation
- Ability to process large applicant pools

Risks:
- Historical hiring bias reflected in training data
- Over-reliance on automated screening
- Exclusion of non-traditional candidates

Best practices:
- Regular bias audits
- Human-in-the-loop decision-making
- Transparent scoring criteria
- Diverse training datasets
